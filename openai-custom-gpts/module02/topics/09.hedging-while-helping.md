# Hedging While Helping: Collaborative Decision-Making

## Key Learning Points

### Problem with Direct Answers
- **User Disengagement**: Bold answers at the top encourage users to "check out"
- **Reduced Thinking**: Users may skip supporting information and reasoning
- **Over-Dependence**: Makes it too easy for users to avoid critical thinking

### Better Approach: Information First
- **Present Evidence**: Show relevant quotations and supporting information first
- **Additional Considerations**: Provide extra context for users to think about
- **Human Support**: Direct users to human experts when needed
- **Collaborative Process**: Work together rather than replace user thinking

### Implementation Strategy
1. **Relevant Information**: Present direct quotations from documents first
2. **Additional Considerations**: Give users extra things to think about
3. **Human Contact**: Provide escape valve to human experts
4. **Encourage Verification**: Tell users to verify information exists in policy

### Benefits of Collaborative Approach
- **User Engagement**: Keeps users thinking and reasoning
- **Better Decisions**: Joint decision-making leads to better outcomes
- **Reduced Risk**: Multiple perspectives reduce chance of errors
- **Learning**: Users understand the reasoning process

### Aviation Analogy
- **Pilot and Co-Pilot**: Both check each other for better decisions
- **No Single Point of Failure**: Multiple entities must make mistakes for major problems
- **Collaborative Safety**: Working together improves outcomes

## Key Takeaway
Instead of giving direct answers that allow users to "check out," present information first and encourage collaborative decision-making. This approach keeps users engaged, improves decision quality, and reduces the risk of over-dependence on AI.
