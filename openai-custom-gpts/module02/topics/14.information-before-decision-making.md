# Information Before Decision-Making: Preventing Hallucination

## Key Learning Points

### Core Problem: Hallucination Risk
- **Missing Information**: GPT tries to solve problems without sufficient context
- **Convincing but Wrong**: Responses may look accurate but be factually incorrect
- **Poor Quality**: Inadequate information leads to subpar answers

### Solution: Information Collection First
- **Gather Context**: Collect comprehensive information before problem-solving
- **User Preferences**: Understand learning style and interaction preferences
- **Past Interactions**: Consider history outside current conversation
- **Desired Outcomes**: Clarify response format and depth preferences

### Types of Information to Collect
1. **Problem Context**: Full scope of where the problem occurs
2. **User Preferences**: Learning style, interaction model, desired format
3. **Past Interactions**: Previous conversations with bot or human experts
4. **Desired Outcome**: Short/concise vs. verbose, list vs. paragraph format

### Implementation Strategy
- **Explicit Instructions**: Tell GPT to collect information first
- **Don't Assume**: Avoid jumping to conclusions
- **Restate and Confirm**: Make information explicit and get user agreement
- **Information Before Action**: Only solve problems after gathering context

### Best Practices
- Always ensure sufficient information before problem-solving
- Make information collection explicit in GPT instructions
- Restate collected information for user confirmation
- Don't jump the gun or make assumptions

## Key Takeaway
Generative AI should always collect sufficient information before attempting to solve problems. This prevents hallucination, improves response quality, and ensures the GPT has the context needed to provide accurate and useful assistance.
