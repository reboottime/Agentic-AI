## Recap

### The Problem

- There are two parties in a GPT:
  - the user who seeks for help
  - the LLM that handles user's input.

These two parties communicate through copy. The LLM output quality primarily depends on two dimensions:

- the user's input
- the LLM's capability to solve the problem

- The GPT's LLM performance is shaped by several factors:
  - Knowledge base: pre-trained knowledge and any additional content provided by its creators.
  - Innate capability: reasoning, comprehension, and problem-solving capacity developed during training.
  - Extended capabilities: tools, plugins, or external resources that supplement its core knowledge.
  - Problem understanding: the degree to which it correctly interprets the user’s request. This depends both on its innate capability and the clarity of the user’s description.

- analyze from LLM perspective:
  - A LLM has its built in Knowledge from training and may have GPT creators supplement knowledge
  - An LLM's result when processing a user's request depends on several factors:
    - its innate capability built during the training stage
    - its extended capabilities provided by its tools and GPT creator's provided knowledge
    - its understanding of the problem, which may be limited by the user's input. The understanding to the problem depends on two factors: its innate capability and how well the user described its problem
- The user also influences the interaction outcome:
  - Innate capability: their understanding of the problem and their ability to communicate it.
  - Instruction-following: users may not always know what to provide, how to provide it, or which steps to follow.
  - Intentions: not all interactions are made in good faith; some may be adversarial or careless.

So we need to consider the operational boundaries the GPT runs within and also how to react when things or users hit the boundaries.

### The Solution

- get clarity (handle ambiguity) from user's end to understand the problem
  - provide refined question on top of user question to get clarity from user end(refinement question pattern)
  - break user's question into sub-questions to get clarity (cognitive verifier)
- Protect ourself from risk while being helpful
  - do not provide unreliable answers, instead, being helpful via providing alternative solutions that are reliable to hedge the risk of GPT (alternative solutions)
- concept mapping:
  - the problem: the same concept may have different interpretation or the concept inputted by user may not be in the knowledge base the GPT has
  - the solution: detect concept ambiguity first. If there are concepts have multiple possible mapping, state that the answer is unclear and suggest possible questions that you could answer, instead of making assumptions
